## Q1　Laws of indices

Simplify $\dfrac{(2x^4)^3}{2x^5}$.

$$(2x^4)^3 = 2^3 \cdot x^{12} = 8x^{12}$$

$$\frac{8x^{12}}{2x^5} = 4x^{12-5} = 4x^7$$

**Answer: C　$4x^7$**

## Q2　Identities

Evaluate $(4x+y)^2-(4x-y)^2$.

Use $a^2-b^2=(a+b)(a-b)$ (no need to expand):

$$\left[(4x+y)-(4x-y)\right]\left[(4x+y)+(4x-y)\right] = (2y)(8x) = 16xy$$

**Answer: D　$16xy$**

## Q3　Comparing coefficients

If $p$ and $q$ are constants such that $x^2+p \equiv (x+2)(x+q)+10$, find $p$.

$$(x+2)(x+q)+10 = x^2+(q+2)x+2q+10$$

Compare coefficients of $x$ (LHS has no $x$ term):

$$q+2 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; q=-2$$

Compare constant terms:

$$p = 2q+10 = 2(-2)+10 = 6$$

**Answer: C　$p=6$**

## Q4　Factor theorem

Given that \(x^3 + 4x^2 + kx - 12\) is divisible by \(x + 3\), find the value of \(k\).

By the factor theorem, \(f(-3) = 0\).

\[
(-3)^3 + 4(-3)^2 - 3k - 12 = 0
\]

\[
-27 + 36 - 3k - 12 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; -3 - 3k = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; k = -1
\]

**Answer: B　\(k = -1\)**

Let $m+2n+6 = 2m-n = 7$, solve for $n$.

Separate into two equations:

$$m+2n = 1 \quad (1)$$
$$2m-n = 7 \quad (2)$$

$(1)\times 2 - (2)$:

$$5n = -5 \;\Rightarrow\; n=-1$$

## Q7　Compound inequalities ("or")

Solve the compound inequality \(15 + 4x < 3\) or \(9 - 2x > 1\).

For the first inequality:
\[15 + 4x < 3 \implies 4x < -12 \implies x < -3\]

For the second inequality:
\[9 - 2x > 1 \implies -2x > -8 \implies x < 4\]

The "or" operation means we take the union of the two sets, which is:
\[x < 4\]

**Answer: C　$x < 4$**

## Q10　Variation

$y$ varies directly with $x^2$ and inversely with $x$ (partially varies). When $x=1$, $y=-4$; when $x=2$, $y=5$. Find $y$ when $x=-2$.

Let $y = k_1 x^2 + \dfrac{k_2}{x}$:

$$k_1 + k_2 = -4 \quad (1)$$
$$4k_1 + \frac{k_2}{2} = 5 \;\Rightarrow\; 8k_1 + k_2 = 10 \quad (2)$$

$(2)-(1)$: $7k_1 = 14 \Rightarrow k_1=2,\ k_2=-6$

$$x=-2:\quad y = 2(-2)^2 + \frac{-6}{-2} = 8 + 3 = 11$$

**Answer: D　$y=11$**

## Q11　Average rate trap

**Stem:** Mary types for 7 hours: the first 3 hours at an average of 63 words per minute, and the next 4 hours at an average of 56 words per minute. Find her average speed over the entire period.

**Trap:** Do not directly average the two rates $(63+56)/2$. Instead, use the total number of words divided by the total time.

$$\text{Average} = \frac{3 \times 63 \times 60 + 4 \times 56 \times 60}{7 \times 60} = \frac{189 + 224}{7} = \frac{413}{7} = 59 \text{ words per minute}$$

**Answer: C　59 words per minute**

## Q14　Bounds of measurement error

The length of the rope is measured as 25 m (accurate to the nearest meter), and it is cut into $n$ segments, with each segment measured as 5 cm (accurate to the nearest centimeter). Find the maximum possible value of $n$.

Convert all units to centimeters:

$$2450 \le L < 2550, \qquad 4.5 \le \ell < 5.5$$

The maximum value of $n$ is obtained by dividing the total length upper limit by the segment lower limit:

$$n < \frac{2550}{4.5} = 566.\dot{6} \;\Rightarrow\; n_{\max} = 566$$

(Verification: $567 \times 4.5 = 2551.5 > 2550$, which is not possible.)

**Answer: B　566**

## Q15　Pythagoras for area

> Figure: Quadrilateral $ABCD$, $\angle A = 90^\circ$; $BC=26$ cm; $\angle ADC=90^\circ$, $CD=6$ cm, $AD=8$ cm.

$\triangle ACD$ (right at $D$):

$$AC = \sqrt{6^2+8^2} = \sqrt{100} = 10$$

$\triangle ABC$ (right at $A$, hypotenuse $BC=26$):

$$AB = \sqrt{26^2-10^2} = \sqrt{576} = 24$$

$$\text{Area} = \frac12(24)(10) + \frac12(6)(8) = 120+24 = 144\text{ cm}^2$$

**Answer: A　144 cm²**

## Q16　Sector arc length

**Stem:** In the diagram, $OAB$ and $OCD$ are two sectors with the same center and the same central angle. The arc length $\overset{\frown}{AB} = 12\pi$ cm and $\overset{\frown}{CD} = 16\pi$ cm, and $OA = 30$ cm.

The arc length is directly proportional to the radius:

$$\frac{OC}{OA} = \frac{16\pi}{12\pi} \;\Rightarrow\; OC = 30 \times \frac{16}{12} = 40$$

$$AC = OC - OA = 40 - 30 = 10 \text{ cm}$$

**Answer: B　10 cm**

## Q17　Parallelogram area (same height)

**Stem:** In parallelogram $ABCD$, $E$ is on $AB$ and $F$ is on $CD$. The extension of $AD$ intersects the extension of $EF$ at $G$. Given $DF:FC=3:4$ and $AD:DG=1:1$, the area of $\triangle DFG$ is $3$ cm².

**Solution:**

Key: $\triangle DFG$ has $DF$ as its base and is of the same height as the parallelogram $ABCD$ (between the same pair of parallel lines). Let this height be $h$.

Since $DF:FC = 3:4$, we have $DF = \dfrac{3}{7}DC$.

$$[\triangle DFG] = \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{3}{7}DC \times h = 3 \;\Rightarrow\; DC \cdot h = 14$$

$$[ABCD] = DC \times h = 14 \text{ cm}^2$$

**Answer: B　14 cm²**

## Q18　Trigonometry: two right triangles

**Stem:** In the diagram, \(D\) is on \(AC\), \(BD \perp AC\); \(\angle BCD = \alpha\) and \(\angle DBA = \beta\); \(BC = \ell\).

**Solution:**

In \(\triangle BDC\) (right-angled at \(D\)):

\[ BD = \ell \sin \alpha \]

In \(\triangle BDA\) (right-angled at \(D\), \(\beta\) is the angle between \(BD\) and \(AB\)):

\[ \cos \beta = \frac{BD}{AB} \]

\[ \therefore AB = \frac{BD}{\cos \beta} = \frac{\ell \sin \alpha}{\cos \beta} \]

**Answer: A \(\dfrac{\ell \sin \alpha}{\cos \beta}\)**

## Q19　Trig identity simplification

$$\frac{\cos 60^\circ}{1-\cos(90^\circ-\theta)}+\frac{\cos 240^\circ}{1-\cos(270^\circ-\theta)}$$

First, use the special angle identities and trigonometric identities:

$$\cos 60^\circ = \frac{1}{2},\quad \cos 240^\circ = -\frac{1}{2},\quad \cos(90^\circ-\theta) = \sin\theta,\quad \cos(270^\circ-\theta) = -\sin\theta$$

Substitute these into the expression:

$$\frac{\frac{1}{2}}{1-\sin\theta} - \frac{\frac{1}{2}}{1+\sin\theta} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{(1+\sin\theta)-(1-\sin\theta)}{1-\sin^2\theta} = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos^2\theta}$$

$$= \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta} \cdot \frac{1}{\cos\theta} = \frac{\tan\theta}{\cos\theta}$$

**Answer: C　$\dfrac{\tan\theta}{\cos\theta}$**

## Q23　Polar coordinates + rotation

**Stem:** Point \( P(-3, -3\sqrt{3}) \) is rotated 90 degrees counterclockwise about the origin. Find the polar coordinates of the image.

**Solution:**

First, calculate the distance \( OP \) from the origin to point \( P \):

\[
OP = \sqrt{(-3)^2 + (-3\sqrt{3})^2} = \sqrt{9 + 27} = 6
\]

Point \( P \) is in the third quadrant. The reference angle for \( P \) is:

\[
\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{-3\sqrt{3}}{-3}\right) = 60^\circ
\]

Since \( P \) is in the third quadrant, the polar angle \( \theta \) is:

\[
\theta = 180^\circ + 60^\circ = 240^\circ
\]

After rotating 90 degrees counterclockwise, the new polar angle \( \theta' \) is:

\[
\theta' = 240^\circ + 90^\circ = 330^\circ
\]

The length \( OP \) remains unchanged.

**Answer: D \((6, 330^\circ)\)**

## Q25　Straight line graph judgment

**Stem:** The graph of $L_1: ax + y = b$ slopes from the lower left to the upper right, and its y-intercept is positive. The graph of $L_2: cx + y = d$ is steeper and its x-intercept is on the positive x-axis (i.e., its y-intercept $d < 0$).

Rewrite in slope-intercept form: $L_1: y = -ax + b$ and $L_2: y = -cx + d$.

- I: $L_1$ slopes upwards → $-a > 0 \Rightarrow a < 0$ ✔
- II: $L_2$ is steeper → $-c > -a \Rightarrow c < a$, so $a < c$ is incorrect ✘
- III: $b > 0 > d$ ✔
- IV: $a < 0, d < 0 \Rightarrow ad > 0$; $b > 0, c < 0 \Rightarrow bc < 0$ → $ad > bc$ ✔

**Answer: C　I, III and IV only**

## Q26　Circle and axes position

**Stem:** The circle has its center at $(h, k)$ in the second quadrant, passes through the $y$-axis, but does not touch the $x$-axis (the entire circle is above the $x$-axis).

**Solution:**

The circle passes through the $y$-axis → $r > -h$; the circle does not touch the $x$-axis → $k - r > 0$, which means $r < k$.

Combining these, we get: $-h < r < k$

- I: $-h < k \Rightarrow h + k > 0$ ✔
- II: $r > h$ (or $r > 0 > h$) → $r - h > 0$ ✔
- III: $r < k$ → $r - k > 0$ ✘

**Answer: A　I and II only**

## Q27　Probability: divisibility

A number $9\star\blacklozenge$ is a three-digit number where $\star$ and $\blacklozenge$ each take values from 0 to 9. Find the probability that this number is divisible by 5.

Total number of possibilities: $10 \times 10 = 100$.

A number is divisible by 5 if and only if its last digit $\blacklozenge \in \{0, 5\}$. There are $10 \times 2 = 20$ such possibilities.

$$P = \frac{20}{100} = \frac{1}{5}$$

**Answer: A　$\frac{1}{5}$**

## Q30　Mean + median constraints

**Stem:** The data set is $19, 10, 12, 12, 13, 13, 14, 15, 16, m, n$ (11 numbers). The mean and median of the data set are both 14.

**Mean:** The sum of the numbers is $11 \times 14 = 154$. We know the sum of the first nine numbers is 124.

$$m + n = 30 \quad (\text{III ✔})$$

**Median:** The median (the 6th number) is 14. We know there are 5 numbers less than 14 ($10, 12, 12, 13, 13$), so $m$ and $n$ cannot be less than 14:

$$m \ge 14 \quad (\text{I ✔}) \;\Rightarrow\; n = 30 - m \le 16 \quad (\text{II ✔})$$

**Answer: D　I, II and III**

## Q31　H.C.F. and L.C.M.

The H.C.F. of the three numbers is $ab^2$ and the L.C.M. is $4a^4b^5c^6$. The first two numbers are $2a^2b^4c$ and $4a^4b^2c^6$. We need to find the third number.

Rule: H.C.F. takes the **minimum** value of the exponents of each letter; L.C.M. takes the **maximum** value.

- $a$: $\min(2,4,a_3)=1 \Rightarrow a_3=1$ (and $\max(2,4,1)=4$ ✔)
- $b$: $\max(4,2,b_3)=5 \Rightarrow b_3=5$ (and $\min(4,2,5)=2$ ✔)
- $c$: $\min(1,6,c_3)=0 \Rightarrow c_3=0$ (and $\max(1,6,0)=6$ ✔)

$$\text{The third number} = ab^5$$

**Answer: B　$ab^5$**

## Q35　Complex numbers

The given expression is \(i^3(\beta i - 3)\).

First, simplify \(i^3\):
\[i^3 = i^2 \cdot i = -1 \cdot i = -i\]

Now substitute \(i^3\) back into the expression:
\[-i(\beta i - 3)\]

Next, distribute \(-i\):
\[-i \cdot \beta i + (-i) \cdot (-3) = -\beta i^2 + 3i\]

Since \(i^2 = -1\), substitute \(-1\) for \(i^2\):
\[-\beta(-1) + 3i = \beta + 3i\]

**Answer: A　\(\beta + 3i\)**

## Q37　Arithmetic sequence — three statements

Given:
\[ a_{18} = 26 \]
\[ a_{23} = 61 \]

**Step 1: Calculate the common difference \( d \)**

The common difference \( d \) is given by:
\[ d = \frac{a_{23} - a_{18}}{23 - 18} = \frac{61 - 26}{5} = 7 \]

**Step 2: Calculate the first term \( a_1 \)**

Using the formula for the \( n \)-th term of an arithmetic sequence \( a_n = a_1 + (n-1)d \):
\[ a_{18} = a_1 + 17d \]
\[ 26 = a_1 + 17 \times 7 \]
\[ 26 = a_1 + 119 \]
\[ a_1 = 26 - 119 = -93 \]

**Step 3: Verify each statement**

- **Statement I**: \( a_{14} = a_{18} - 4d \)
\[ a_{14} = 26 - 4 \times 7 = 26 - 28 = -2 \]
Since \( -2 < 0 \), Statement I is true.

- **Statement II**: \( a_1 - a_2 = -d \)
\[ a_2 = a_1 + d = -93 + 7 = -86 \]
\[ a_1 - a_2 = -93 - (-86) = -93 + 86 = -7 \]
Since \( -7 < 0 \), Statement II is true.

- **Statement III**: \( S_{27} = \frac{27}{2}(a_1 + a_{27}) \)
First, find \( a_{27} \):
\[ a_{27} = a_1 + 26d = -93 + 26 \times 7 = -93 + 182 = 89 \]
Now, calculate \( S_{27} \):
\[ S_{27} = \frac{27}{2}(-93 + 89) = \frac{27}{2}(-4) = -54 \]
Since \( -54 \) is not greater than \( 0 \), Statement III is false.

**Answer: A　I and II only**

## Q39　Sine/cosine graph

**Stem:** The graph of a sine or cosine function is shown. The curve reaches its maximum value of 7 at \(x=0\) and its minimum value of 1 at \(x=90\). It returns to 7 at \(x=180\).

**Solution:**

The difference between the maximum and minimum values is \(7 - 1 = 6\). Therefore, the amplitude is \(\frac{6}{2} = 3\). The midline is \(\frac{7 + 1}{2} = 4\).

The maximum value occurs at \(x=0\) and \(x=180\), indicating a period of \(180^\circ\). This corresponds to a cosine function with a period of \(180^\circ\), which is \(\cos 2x^\circ\).

Thus, the equation of the curve is:
\[ y = 4 + 3\cos 2x^\circ \]

(Verification: At \(x=90\), \(4 + 3\cos 180^\circ = 1\), which is correct.)

**Answer: D　\(y=4+3\cos 2x^\circ\)**

## Q40　Dihedral angle of regular tetrahedron

In a regular tetrahedron \(ABCD\) with edge length \(a\), find the dihedral angle between planes \(ABC\) and \(BCD\).

Let \(M\) be the midpoint of \(BC\): \(AM \perp BC\) and \(DM \perp BC\). Therefore, the dihedral angle is \(\angle AMD\).

\[
AM = DM = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}a, \quad AD = a
\]

\[
\cos\angle AMD = \frac{AM^2 + DM^2 - AD^2}{2(AM)(DM)} = \frac{\frac{3}{4}a^2 + \frac{3}{4}a^2 - a^2}{2 \cdot \frac{3}{4}a^2} = \frac{\frac{1}{2}a^2}{\frac{3}{2}a^2} = \frac{1}{3}
\]

\[
\angle AMD \approx 70.5^\circ \approx 71^\circ
\]

**Answer: D　\(71^\circ\)**

## Q41　Tangent + semicircle (hard)

1. $\angle PBQ = 90^\circ$ (angle in a semicircle) → $\angle BQP = 78^\circ$ in $\triangle PBQ$
2. $OB = OQ$ (radii of the semicircle) → $\angle QBO = 78^\circ$ and $\angle BOQ = 24^\circ$
3. $\angle OBC = \angle QBC - \angle QBO = 90^\circ - 78^\circ = 12^\circ$
4. Alternate segment theorem: $\angle POC = \angle OBC = 12^\circ$
5. In $\triangle OPC$: $\angle OPC = 12^\circ$ (since $BCP$ is a straight line) = $\angle POC$ → $\angle PCO = 156^\circ$ → $\angle BCO = 180^\circ - 156^\circ = 24^\circ$
6. In $\triangle OBC$: $\angle BOC = 180^\circ - 12^\circ - 24^\circ = 144^\circ$
7. $A, B, O, C$ are concyclic (cyclic quadrilateral) → $\angle BAC + \angle BOC = 180^\circ$ (opposite angles are supplementary)

$$\angle BAC = 180^\circ - 144^\circ = 36^\circ$$

**Answer: C　$36^\circ$**

## Q42　Line intersecting a circle

To find the range of \( k \) values such that the circle \( x^2 + y^2 + 2x - 4y - 13 = 0 \) intersects the line \( x - y + k = 0 \) at two distinct points, we need to follow these steps:

1. **Find the center and radius of the circle:**

   The given circle equation is:
   \[
   x^2 + y^2 + 2x - 4y - 13 = 0
   \]

   We complete the square for both \( x \) and \( y \):

   For \( x \):
   \[
   x^2 + 2x = (x + 1)^2 - 1
   \]

   For \( y \):
   \[
   y^2 - 4y = (y - 2)^2 - 4
   \]

   Substituting these into the circle equation:
   \[
   (x + 1)^2 - 1 + (y - 2)^2 - 4 - 13 = 0
   \]
   Simplifying:
   \[
   (x + 1)^2 + (y - 2)^2 - 18 = 0
   \]
   \[
   (x + 1)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 18
   \]

   Therefore, the center of the circle is \((-1, 2)\) and the radius \( r \) is:
   \[
   r = \sqrt{18} = 3\sqrt{2}
   \]

2. **Calculate the distance from the center of the circle to the line:**

   The line equation is:
   \[
   x - y + k = 0
   \]

   The distance \( d \) from a point \((x_1, y_1)\) to a line \( Ax + By + C = 0 \) is given by:
   \[
   d = \frac{|Ax_1 + By_1 + C|}{\sqrt{A^2 + B^2}}
   \]

   Here, \( A = 1 \), \( B = -1 \), \( C = k \), and the point \((x_1, y_1) = (-1, 2)\). Substituting these values:
   \[
   d = \frac{|1(-1) + (-1)(2) + k|}{\sqrt{1^2 + (-1)^2}} = \frac{|-1 - 2 + k|}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{|k - 3|}{\sqrt{2}}
   \]

3. **Determine the condition for two distinct intersection points:**

   For the circle and the line to intersect at two distinct points, the distance \( d \) must be less than the radius \( r \):
   \[
   d < r
   \]
   Substituting the values of \( d \) and \( r \):
   \[
   \frac{|k - 3|}{\sqrt{2}} < 3\sqrt{2}
   \]

   Multiply both sides by \(\sqrt{2}\):
   \[
   |k - 3| < 6
   \]

   This inequality can be solved by considering the two cases:
   \[
   -6 < k - 3 < 6
   \]

   Adding 3 to all parts of the inequality:
   \[
   -3 < k < 9
   \]

**Answer: B　\(-3 < k < 9\)**

## Q43　Combinations: at least one girl

12 boys and 8 girls, select a team of 5 people and **at least one girl**, find the number of combinations.

## Q44　Probability: drawing balls (without replacement)

A bag contains six balls numbered $7, 8, 8, 9, 9, 9$. Balls are drawn without replacement until a $9$ is drawn. Find the probability of drawing exactly three balls.

Exactly three draws ⇔ the first two are not $9$ and the third is $9$:

$$P = \frac{3}{6} \times \frac{2}{5} \times \frac{3}{4} = \frac{3}{20}$$

**Answer: D　$\dfrac{3}{20}$**

## Q45　Invariance of statistics

The average, range, and variance of the set \(\{x_1, \dots, x_{100}\}\) are \(m_1\), \(r_1\), and \(v_1\) respectively. After adding \(m_1\) to the set, the new statistics are \(m_2\), \(r_2\), and \(v_2\). Which of the following must be true?

- **I: \(m_2 = \dfrac{100m_1 + m_1}{101} = m_1\) ✔**
- **II: \(m_1\) is between \(\min\) and \(\max\) → Range remains unchanged \(r_1 = r_2\) ✔**
- III: \(v_2 = \dfrac{100v_1 + (m_1 - m_1)^2}{101} = \dfrac{100}{101}v_1 < v_1\) (unless \(v_1 = 0\)) ✘

**Answer: A　I and II only**
